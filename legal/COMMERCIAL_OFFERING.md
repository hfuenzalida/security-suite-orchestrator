## When you might want a separate commercial arrangement

Organizations often still need **one or more** of the following (none of which are included in the MIT License grant by default):

| Need | Typical approach |
|------|------------------|
| **Support SLA** | Paid support agreement with response times and escalation |
| **Indemnification / liability cap** | Negotiated **separate contract** (not in MIT) |
| **Warranty or fitness for a purpose** | **Separate warranty** statement, if offered |
| **Custom license terms** | **Dual licensing**: MIT for OSS; proprietary or alternate terms for specific use cases |
| **Training / integration** | Professional services statement of work |

## Relationship to repository documents

- **[TERMS_OF_USE.md](./TERMS_OF_USE.md)** — Template terms; commercial customers typically use a **master services agreement** or **order form** instead or in addition.
- **[PRIVACY_NOTICE.md](./PRIVACY_NOTICE.md)** — Baseline for local/OSS use; a **hosted** product or **support** that processes customer data needs a **privacy policy** and (where required) **DPA** drafted for your processing activities.

## Dual licensing (optional pattern)

If you offer **proprietary** or **alternate** terms for certain deployments:

1. Keep **MIT** as the default for the public tree **or** clearly mark which files are MIT vs proprietary add-ons.
2. Provide a **written commercial license** (PDF/Docusign) that states scope, fees, term, and any **patent/trademark** grants you intend to give.
3. Ensure **trademark** use: the MIT License does **not** grant your brand name; set a **trademark policy** if you care about forks using your name.
